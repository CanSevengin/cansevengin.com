I work in international sales, which means a lot of my week is spent on questions like: who are the right importers in this country, how do they buy, what do they already carry, and why would they take a meeting with us?

AI has changed how I answer those questions more than any tool I've used in my career. But the changes are uneven, and it's worth being precise about where.

## What got dramatically faster

**Market mapping.** Building a first picture of a new country used to take days of searching, translating and copying into spreadsheets. Now a research workflow can produce a structured first draft of the landscape in an afternoon: the main retail chains, the likely importers, the trade fairs that matter, the regulatory basics. It still needs checking, but checking is much faster than starting from zero.

**Working across languages.** Reading a distributor's website in Spanish, a regulation summary in German or a marketplace listing in Arabic is no longer a barrier. Neither is replying in a tone that feels natural rather than translated. For a team selling into dozens of countries, this alone is a huge shift.

**Preparation.** Before a call, I can have a short brief on the company, its brands, its recent news and the questions worth asking. Ten minutes of preparation now covers what used to take an hour.

**First drafts of everything.** Proposals, follow-ups, product sheets, fair invitations. The draft is rarely the final version, but it's never a blank page.

## What didn't change at all

**Trust.** A distributor commits money, warehouse space and their own reputation to your brand. They decide based on whether they believe you will deliver, support them and still be there in three years. No model builds that for you.

**The relationship itself.** The deal often moves forward because of a conversation at a fair stand, a quick answer on a Sunday, or remembering what the buyer said about their market last time. AI can remind you; it can't care on your behalf.

**Judgement about fit.** A list of fifty possible partners is useful. Knowing which three are worth a flight is still the job.

## The new risk: everyone sounds the same

If every exporter uses the same tools to write the same polished cold email, buyers learn to ignore all of them. The advantage shifts back to things that are hard to automate: a specific insight about their market, a reference they recognise, a meeting in person.

So my rule is simple. Use AI to remove the hours of groundwork, then spend those hours on the human part. Faster research is only valuable if it buys you more real conversations.
