If your phone is three or four years old and still holds a charge through the day, you probably do not need to upgrade this autumn. The new phones are better, but mostly in ways a busy person will barely notice. The real reasons to upgrade are boring ones: a dying battery, a phone that no longer gets updates, or one specific feature you will use every day.

Here is what actually launched, and the four checks I would run before spending the money.

## What launched this autumn

Apple announced the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max on 9 September, and they went on sale on 18 September, in Türkiye as well. In the US they cost $100 more than the equivalent iPhone 17 Pro models. In Türkiye, the 256 GB iPhone 18 Pro starts at 137,999 TL and the Pro Max at 149,999 TL.

The same event introduced Apple's first foldable, the iPhone Duo. Pre-orders open on 16 October and it launches on 23 October, starting at $1,999 in the US.

On the Android side, the big autumn names arrived a little earlier. Google's Pixel 11 series was announced in August, starting at $899 in the US. Samsung's Galaxy S26 FE was announced at the end of August and went on sale in the US in early September at $699.

## Check 1: Is your battery actually the problem?

Battery is the most honest reason to upgrade. Apple's own numbers show a real but modest step: up to 36 hours of video playback on the iPhone 18 Pro, against 33 hours on the 17 Pro, and up to 45 hours on the Pro Max, against 39. Apple also says the Pro Max has the largest battery ever in an iPhone.

That matters if you are coming from a much older phone. If you are coming from last year's model, three to six extra hours of video playback will not change your week. And if your current phone is fine except for a worn battery, a battery replacement is usually the cheaper fix. Check your battery health in settings before you look at new phones.

## Check 2: Will the camera change what you shoot?

The headline camera feature on the iPhone 18 Pro is a variable aperture on the main 48 MP camera, a first for the iPhone. Six blades adjust the aperture automatically, and you can set it manually from f/1.48 to f/4.0.

This is a genuine upgrade for people who care about depth of field and want more manual control. For someone who mostly photographs kids, food, documents and the occasional sunset, the automatic mode was already very good. I would not upgrade for this alone unless photography is a real hobby.

## Check 3: Do the AI features need new hardware?

This is where it gets interesting, and a little confusing. The headline AI feature this year is the new Siri in iOS 27. But it does not require the new phone: according to MacRumors, Siri AI works on the iPhone 15 Pro and newer. If you already have one of those, you get the main AI upgrade for free.

Google's Pixel 11 brings Gemini Intelligence, which can carry out tasks on its own. But Android Central notes that this task-handling is limited to the US. If you live in Türkiye like me, that is worth knowing before you pay for it.

My rule: buy a phone for what it does in your country today, not for features that may arrive later.

## Check 4: How long will your current phone keep getting updates?

This is the check most people skip, and it is the most important for security. iOS 27, released on 14 September, supports the same iPhones as iOS 26: the iPhone 11 series, from 2019, and newer, plus the second-generation iPhone SE and later. So if you have an iPhone 11 or newer, you are still supported this year.

On Android, Google's Pixel 11 comes with seven years of software updates and seven years of spare parts. A long, written support promise is one of the best reasons to choose a specific phone, because it decides how long the phone is safe to use.

## What about the foldable?

The iPhone Duo is the most exciting launch, and the one I would be most careful with. It has a 5.4-inch outer screen and a 7.6-inch inner screen. It also comes with trade-offs: at 254 grams it is the heaviest iPhone, it uses Touch ID instead of Face ID, and it has no telephoto camera. It is a first-generation product at $1,999. If you are curious, wait for real-world reviews about the crease and durability before you commit.

## My verdict

Upgrade this year if your battery no longer lasts the day, if your phone has dropped out of update support, or if you shoot enough photos to benefit from the new camera. Otherwise, keep your phone, replace the battery if needed, and enjoy the free iOS 27 or Android update. The best phone is often the one you already paid for.
